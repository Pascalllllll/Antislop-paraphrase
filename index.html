    What gets crossed out

    Each rule targets a pattern that shows up far more in machine-written text than in writing people do by hand. Every rule can be switched off under Rules to apply.

    1. Em dashes

      Paired dashes become commas. A single dash before a short ending becomes a colon. Double hyphens count too.

      The policy —, announced without warning —, affects everyone.

    2. Chatbot lines and openers

      "Certainly! Here's a rewritten version:" at the top, "I hope this helps!" at the bottom, and theatrical openers like "Here's the thing:" in between.

      Here's the thing: Mmost teams never read the report.

    3. Buzzwords

      Delve, seamless, robust, leverage, testament, tapestry. Swapped for the plain word, or dropped when it only decorates a noun.

      Our world-class team will leverage use the data.

    4. Filler and hedges

      "In order to" becomes "to". "Could potentially" becomes "could". Throat-clearing like "It's important to note that" goes.

      It's important to note that Tthis could potentially could slip.

    5. Formula sentences

      "It's not just X, it's Y." "Not only X but also Y." A pile of Moreover and Furthermore. The upbeat send-off at the end.

      It's not just a dashboard, it's and a command center.

    6. Formatting tells

      Emoji in headings, **bold** markers on every term, sentences shouted in capitals, and clusters of "scare" "quotes".

      🚀 Launch plan. We must act NOW BEFORE FRIDAY now before Friday.

    What it can't do

    This is a set of rules, not a language model. It swaps and cuts phrases it recognizes. It does not rethink your argument, fix a passive voice with no actor, break up a rule-of-three list, or catch a buzzword it has never seen. Some edits can land awkwardly, so read the result before you use it. The marked view shows every change.

    Runs in your browser. No account, no cookies, no analytics, no network requests after the page loads. Up to 150,000 words per run.
